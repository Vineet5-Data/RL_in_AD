## 2. Steelman of Decoder-Light (updated — now empirically grounded, not purely theoretical)

- **The free-bits arithmetic was real** (re-verified from the prior pass): with `STOCH_GROUPS=16` and DreamerV3's documented 0.5/0.1 KL-balance weights, a fully-floored 16-group posterior gives exactly `0.5·16·1.0 + 0.1·16·1.0 = 9.6` — the pinned value seen in Run 2′ and at Run 3's R1 checkpoint. This diagnostic-precision point was correct on its own terms even before Run 1 existed.
- **The controlled experiment now exists and confirms the causal claim, not just the arithmetic.** `models/world_model2.py`'s `HeadsLight` has "No MDN anywhere" (its own docstring) and `self.gps = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(stoch_dim, hidden), ...)` — the coarse GPS head is **structurally** z-only (cannot see `h` at all, not just discouraged from it via an auxiliary loss). `training/stage2r.py`'s eval log (`eval_run1_roadhead.txt`) shows `kl` (eval-side, summed over 16 groups) ranging 8.5-12.1 across all 15 checkpoints, final = 10.504 (≈0.66/group) — never approaching the near-zero/floor-pinned pattern of Runs 1-3. This is the cleanest single piece of evidence anywhere in the project for `dai_wang_wipf_posterior_collapse_blame.json`'s mechanism: an expressive decoder (the MDN) was creating exactly the kind of local-minima/information-truncation behavior that paper predicts, and removing it — not reweighting it, not adding a competing loss beside it — is what broke the pattern. Change 13 (dropout) and Change 14 (z-only auxiliary *alongside* an intact, `lambda_mdn=1.0`-weighted MDN) never tested this specific lever; that gap is now closed.
- **The prediction result is the strongest P2 evidence in the project.** hit@15 (0.579) ≈ hit@1 (0.586) at the final checkpoint — a materially flatter horizon curve than Run 3's reconstruction RSSM (which peaked at 0.648 hit@1 mid-run and *declined* toward later checkpoints per the MTL-competition finding). Verified against the same protocol: `eval_research2.py`'s header states "identical split... identical conventions: tolerant Hit@1..., T_WARM=8" as `eval_stage2.py` — this is a genuine apples-to-apples comparison, not a redefined, easier metric (see §3, stress-test (b)).
- **MuDreamer's own flagged risk (normalization) was addressed and resolved favorably**, closing `methodology-comparison.md`'s Gap item 5: `_norm_layer` supports `batch`/`layer`/`none` (`--norm batch` default), confirmed in the RSSM's prior/posterior MLPs — training was stable at 15k steps with no constant-code collapse observed, exactly the failure mode MuDreamer's own paper (`mudreamer_no_reconstruction.json`) warned could occur on a different architecture family without it.
- **match@1 was still rising monotonically at 15k** (0.514 → 0.695, no visible plateau in the eval table), with a clean, specific, mechanically-diagnosed cause for the late-run cl_gps regression (cosine LR already ≈0 when the L=64 curriculum switch fired at step 10k) — this is the kind of "one lever, well understood, cheap to test" situation the ladder favors, unlike Stage 2's later rounds where each new intervention required contested interpretation of ambiguous signals.

This is now a genuinely positive result for the world-model half of P2, not merely a promising theory.

## 3. Challenge: Point-by-Point, Rated Against the Record (updated)

### Claim 1 — "The KL~9.6 floor is a free-bits artifact, not proof of a dead latent" → **SUPPORTED (upgraded from Partially Supported)**

The arithmetic was already independently verified correct in the prior pass. Run 1 supplies what was previously missing: a **causal, controlled confirmation**, not just a plausible alternative reading. Same RSSM family (`RSSM2` in `world_model2.py` is a light modification of the original `RSSM`, same GRU + 16×16 categorical latents), same data, same held-out eval split and protocol — only the decoder changed (MDN removed, GPS head made structurally z-only, normalization added) — and `kl_dyn_raw` moved from "decays toward the ~1-nat/group floor" (Run 2′: `stage2_correction.md` line 291, peaked once at ~7.4 nats *total* but never held there; Change 13/Run 3: pinned clamped loss at 9.6 exactly, per-group raw 0.41-0.44 nats) to "climbs to 8-9 nats *total* by step 1500 and holds through 15k, eval-side ≈0.66/group throughout." This is a real, durable, qualitative regime change attributable to the one architectural difference that was changed.

**Caveat that keeps this from being a completely closed question:** confirming the latent isn't collapsed is a necessary, not sufficient, condition for the matching claim. A healthy KL only proves `z` carries *some* nontrivial information relative to `h` — whether that information is the *right kind* (continuous spatial calibration vs. discrete routing) is claim 2, and whether it's *enough* to match Track B is the online/offline question in the verdict and §4. For the thesis's diagnostic narrative specifically (why did Runs 1-3 fail, mechanistically), this claim is now fully supported and should be written up as settled — the free-bits-floor confusion was real, and removing the expressive decoder, not reinterpreting the clamped loss term, is what resolved it.

### Claim 2 — "Fully decoder-free is dangerous: topological shortcut" → **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED (graduated from "plausible, untested" — now has real but ambiguous data)**

The strongest form of the claim (z reduced to a *pure* discrete routing switch, discarding continuous spatial information entirely) is **not** borne out: the z-only GPS decode (`HeadsLight.gps`, `z_flat`-only input, no `h`) achieves `cl_gps` = 0.69 km at the final checkpoint (`eval_run1_roadhead.txt` line 20) — competitive with, in fact slightly *better than*, Run 3's full `h+z+MDN` best of 0.7695 km (`eval_run3_20260709.log`). If `z` had degenerated to a small number of discrete routing bits, a linear/shallow MLP decode from `z` alone should perform *much worse* than a full joint decoder with an expressive MDN residual on top, not comparably or better. This is real evidence against the sharpest version of the hypothesis.

The weaker form of the claim survives, however: 0.69 km (690 m) is nowhere near spatially "calibrated" in any useful sense — it is roughly the scale of several city blocks, far coarser than the road-segment-level precision map matching needs, and nowhere close to the old (never-cleared) 0.15 km gate. `z` carries *some* decodable continuous signal, not zero, but not enough to call the topological-shortcut concern refuted either. The specific ablation new_findings itself proposed to settle this cleanly — **h-only vs. z-only RoadScorer** — was **not run** in Run 1: `RoadScorer` in `world_model2.py` is still jointly wired (`q_proj = nn.Linear(h_dim + stoch_dim, d)`, unchanged from the original), so whether the match@1 = 0.695 gain is coming from `z` newly carrying road-relevant signal or from `h` simply being freed from decoder-competition to focus harder on the (unchanged) road task is genuinely unresolved. Rating stays partial pending that specific, cheap, already-available ablation (§4, listed as a Run-2-adjacent diagnostic, not requiring any new training).

### Claim 3 — "research2 branch partially implements decoder-light (MDN removed, RoadScorer prioritized, LayerNorm added)" → **SUPPORTED (reversed from Contradicted)**

The prior pass rated this Contradicted against the branch's state at that time, and that rating was correct for that state — verified via `git log`/`git show` against the committed history, which showed no MDN removal, no normalization layer, and no z-only structural rewiring anywhere. Since then, real, uncommitted-but-present work has landed: `git status` in `.worktrees/research2` shows `models/world_model2.py`, `training/stage2r.py`, `training/stage3.py`, `training/rewards.py`, `eval_research2.py`, and `ckpt_kaggle_run1/` (with a 185.8 KB training log) all newly present. Direct inspection confirms:
- MDN fully removed (`HeadsLight`, "No MDN anywhere," no `self.mdn` attribute at all — not down-weighted, not present).
- Coarse GPS head structurally z-only (`self.gps` takes only `z_flat`).
- Normalization added and configurable (`_norm_layer` → `BatchNorm1d`/`LayerNorm`/none, `--norm batch` default), directly answering `mudreamer_no_reconstruction.json`'s own flagged uncertainty about whether its BatchNorm requirement transfers to a GRU+categorical-latent architecture — it does, empirically, here.
- RoadScorer *not* restructured (still `(h,z)`-joint) — so "prioritizing RoadScorer" happened at the loss-weighting/training-signal level (road CE is now the dominant/primary supervised signal in the absence of the MDN), not via an architectural change to the RoadScorer itself; the original document's phrasing overstated this specific piece even now, worth a precise correction in the thesis text.
- No stop-gradient probe exists in the current implementation (grep confirms no such mechanism) — the "optional stop-gradient probe if MTL competition persists" piece of the original proposal remains unbuilt, which is fine, since the reward-head/road-CE-vs-GPS-head competition that would have motivated it does not appear to be the dominant remaining issue (the actor's reward-vs-matching tension, §7 finding 3 of `methodology-comparison.md`, is a different competition, between the RL reward and the road head, not between two decoder heads).

### Claim 4 — "Ablation curriculum to prove z health" → **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED, now with real (if incomplete) data instead of a pure proposal**

- **Held-out prior rollouts**: run and reported at all 15 checkpoints, exactly as previously argued this ablation already existed — now with a stronger result (hit@15 ≈ hit@1, flatter than Run 3). Confirmed real and confirmed positive.
- **Z-only GPS probe error**: run and reported (`cl_gps` = 0.69 km z-only decode). Confirmed real; result is ambiguous-but-informative (§ claim 2), not the clean "mathematically guarantees calibration" the original proposal claimed — 0.69 km is evidence of *some* signal, not proof of calibration.
- **Z-only Road Scorer** (mirrors Change 13B's `zonly_road`, which already failed its own gate): **not clearly re-run or re-reported** in the `research2` Run 1 materials inspected (`eval_run1_roadhead.txt`/`eval_run1_actor.txt` report `match@1`/`hit@N`/`cl_gps`/`kl`/`ent`, not a separate zonly_road column) — still an open item.
- **H-only Road Scorer** (the one genuinely novel ablation flagged in the prior pass): still not run. `RoadScorer` remains jointly wired, so there is no direct h-vs-z decomposition of the match@1 = 0.695 result anywhere in the current record.

Net: two of four proposed ablations are now real and reported (with mixed results), two remain outstanding — both are cheap (no retraining, reuse the frozen Run-1 checkpoint) and should be the first thing run before Run 2's GPU budget is spent (§4).

## Stress-Tests (per coordinator's specific questions)

**(a) Online match@1 (0.695) vs. offline HMM Viterbi (0.8447) — is this a fair comparison, and is closing 15pp plausible?**

Not fair as currently reported, and the gap is probably partly an artifact of comparing an online filter to an offline smoother, not purely a representation-quality gap. Evidence for this within the project's own numbers: for the *identical* NK-HMM model, going from offline Viterbi (0.8447, full trajectory, forward-backward-equivalent dynamic programming with future context) to online transition-only prediction (0.5918 at H=1, per `hmm_results.md`'s prediction table) costs **25pp** — for the same emission/transition model, using only the past. If online-vs-offline decoding costs the *classical* model 25pp, it is not safe to attribute the WM's 15pp gap (0.695 vs 0.8447) entirely to representation quality; some, possibly most, of it may be the same online/offline asymmetry. **The single most informative and cheapest next step is not more training — it's adding an offline/Viterbi-style (or simple forward-backward smoothing) decode over the WM's own posterior road-membership sequence**, reusing the already-trained, already-checkpointed Run-1 WM with zero new training. Until that number exists, "matching is conceded to Track B permanently" is not a supportable conclusion — it is one plausible reading among at least two.

**(b) Does the flat hit@15 curve survive scrutiny under the same eval protocol as Run 3's decaying curve?**

Yes, verified directly: `eval_research2.py`'s own header explicitly states "identical split... identical conventions: tolerant Hit@1..., T_WARM=8" matching `eval_stage2.py`, and code inspection confirms the same held-out slice (`SKIP_TRAJS=200_000, N_TRAJS=500`), same `T_WARM=8`, same tolerant-Hit@1 definition (`d_perp <= d1 + 5.0`). This is a genuine like-for-like comparison, not a redefined or loosened metric.

**(c) Is the actor's negative result terminal for P2-as-matching-decoder, or does the reward design just need rework — and is that in-scope?**

Not clearly terminal. The failure mode is structurally explained (entropy collapsed 1.49→0.34 toward a reward-optimum that trades `d_perp` accuracy for heading/topology consistency — the `r_topo`/`r_head` terms in `training/rewards.py`'s weighted sum, per `methodology.md` §2.5, may simply be over-weighted relative to `r_dist` for a *matching*-flavored objective), and it echoes a real pattern already seen elsewhere in this project (Track B's C2/C3: a learned signal loses to raw geometry). A single, bounded reweighting experiment — reduce `lambda_topo`/heading-reward weight relative to `r_dist`, re-run *only* the actor-critic stage (`training/stage3.py`) against the **already-trained, frozen** Run-1/Run-2 WM checkpoint — is cheap (methodology.md §5 already scopes Stage-3-style fine-tuning as "cheapest stage; no new data loading") and answers a real question. It should be **in scope as one bounded, pre-registered attempt**, not open-ended reward engineering: a single reweighting, a fixed short training budget, and a hard stop if it doesn't clear a pre-declared bar (§4). If it still underperforms the road-head readout after that one attempt, write the actor track up as a genuine, final honest negative — "P2's RL-agent-selects-segments component does not currently improve on P2's world-model-representation component for matching" — and note that this specifically complicates `CLAUDE.md`'s P2 definition (an RL agent sequentially selecting segments) and `methodology.md` §2.6's unification claim (same policy for matching and prediction), both of which should be revised in the thesis text to separate "world-model quality" (supported) from "RL-driven segment selection" (not supported, at least for matching, with the current reward design).

**(d) The free-bits/9.6-nats diagnosis — moot now, or still worth rating?**

Still worth rating, and rated SUPPORTED above (claim 1) — not moot, because it is now the *correct, empirically confirmed* account of why Runs 1-3 failed, which belongs in the thesis's methodology chapter as the resolved diagnostic narrative (contested reading → controlled intervention → resolution), directly citing `dai_wang_wipf_posterior_collapse_blame.json` as the theoretical prediction that held.
